## Contract

- Directive names are ASCII case-insensitive. Unknown directives are
  ignored.
- `max-age=N` accepts a `HIT` only while the stored age is at most `N`
  seconds. `max-age=0` always revalidates (or fetches when there is no
  validator).
- `min-fresh=N` requires at least `N` seconds of remaining server freshness;
  a stale entry cannot satisfy a positive value.
- `max-stale[=N]` accepts a stale entry only within `N` seconds of staleness.
  The bare form accepts any staleness. It never overrides `no-cache`,
  `max-age` or `min-fresh`.
- Malformed, negative or overflowing delta-seconds are treated as zero.
  Duplicate directives keep the strictest value (`min` for the three numeric
  directives; a finite `max-stale` beats the unlimited bare form).
- Age is measured since the entry was stored. Origin `Date`/`Age`
  correction remains out of scope and is documented in the ADR.
- The policy lives in the lookup call (and in `CacheFetch` for the h1
  exchange), never in `StoredEntry`: a `max-age=0` client cannot shorten the
  freshness another client receives.
- AUD-01 remains unchanged: a revoking `304` invalidates the entry and
  forces the unconditional full fetch even when the request carried client
  directives; a second `304` to that unconditional request is `502`.

## E2E journeys (real TCP origin, no mocks)

`cargo test -p eqproxy-net --test e2e_cache` → **24 passed, 0 failed**.
The new or strengthened journeys are:

- `client_max_age_zero_revalidates_without_poisoning_the_shared_entry`
  (`e2e_cache.rs:658`): the `max-age=0` request gets `REVALIDATED` and the
  origin sees one conditional; the next anonymous request still gets `HIT`
  from the same entry; `max-age=120` is also a `HIT`.
- `client_min_fresh_revalidates_only_when_the_remaining_lifetime_is_too_short`
  (`:690`): `min-fresh=30` hits the 60 s entry; `min-fresh=120` revalidates.
- `client_max_stale_serves_only_within_the_requested_margin` (`:724`):
  after the entry becomes stale, `max-stale=10` serves it without dialing;
  `max-stale=0` revalidates.
- `max_age_zero_is_per_request_even_for_lock_waiters` (`:762`): the test
  waits until the real origin has registered the owner's request before
  launching the second `max-age=0` client, so that client deterministically
  meets the in-flight cache lock. The owner re-fetches; a normal client still
  receives `HIT` while the owner is at the origin; the waiter fetches under
  its own policy after the lock. The origin sees exactly `warm + owner +
  waiter`, and the replacement remains a `HIT` for later clients.
- `a_second_304_to_the_unconditional_retry_is_a_bad_gateway` (`:840`) now
  sends `max-age=0, min-fresh=60, max-stale=600` and still gets `502` after
  the revoking `304` and the unconditional retry.

The other 20 journeys (hit/miss, `Vary`, clean `ETag` revalidation, TTL,
single-flight, disk restart, bypasses, oversized bodies and pipelining)
remain in the same binary and pass.
